## The two dates

FACT: The Planning &amp; Zoning Commission public hearing on the Kite Farm rezoning is **Tuesday, November 3, 2026 at 6:00 PM**. The City Council hearing is **Tuesday, November 10, 2026 at 7:00 PM**. Both at the Municipal Complex, 311 Burton Hill Road. Source: written answers from the City Administrator, October 6, 2026. (As of October 11 the city's online calendar does not yet list either meeting; agendas will be linked here once posted.)

## What a protest does

FACT: Under Texas Local Government Code § 211.0061, if the owners of at least 20 percent of the land within 200 feet of the property proposed for rezoning sign written protests, the rezoning can only pass with a **three-fourths supermajority of the full city council** instead of a simple majority. Rights-of-way count in the 200-foot area. This is not a petition the council may ignore; it is a vote threshold the law imposes.

FACT: One open question, stated plainly: 2023 legislation (HB 24) sets a higher 60 percent protest threshold for certain residential-development changes under § 211.0061(b)(3). Whether a continuing-care retirement community with assisted living and a public restaurant falls inside or outside that provision is a classification question the city has not answered. The question has been put to the city in writing; the answer will be posted here. File your protest either way. The protest is identical; only the threshold it triggers is in question.

## The city's confirmed procedure

FACT: The city confirmed the mechanics in writing on October 6, 2026:

- Protests are filed with the **City Secretary, Kimberly Biggs**, at 311 Burton Hill Road.
- The city has **no protest form of its own**. It requires "original statements written, signed and received from the landowner."
- The city's stated position on timing: a signed protest is accepted **up to the moment the mayor closes the public hearing** at the City Council meeting, before final action. A protest received before the close of the P&amp;Z public hearing will also be read at that meeting.
- The city provided a list of **47 addresses within 200 feet** of the property, and noted the ownership information is publicly available through the Tarrant Appraisal District.

OPINION: Do not wait for the deadline. The city's "up to the close of the council hearing" answer is more generous than the statute requires, and it is the city's position, not a court's. File before November 3 and your protest is also read into the P&amp;Z record, where the first vote happens.

## How to file, in four steps

FACT: 1. **Check whether you're in the 200-foot area.** If you own property near the Kite Farm tract on White Settlement Road, you likely are; the area includes 47 addresses. Not sure? Use the [email updates form](/#alerts) and we'll help you check against the appraisal district records.

FACT: 2. **Print and sign the protest form**: [Written protest form (PDF)](/files/written-protest-form.pdf). It satisfies the city's requirement of an original written, signed statement from the landowner. Every owner on the deed should sign. Signing for a trust or company? Add your title after your name.

FACT: 3. **Deliver the original** to City Secretary Kimberly Biggs at 311 Burton Hill Road. The form includes an authorization line so a neighbor can hand-deliver it for you if that's easier.

FACT: 4. **Do it before Tuesday, November 3 at 6:00 PM** so it's in the record for the first public hearing.

## And whether or not you own land near the tract

FACT: Public hearings are exactly that: public. Any resident can speak at the November 3 P&amp;Z hearing and the November 10 council hearing, protest rights or not. Submit a Public Comment Form in the lobby before comments begin. The full story of the project, the taxes, and the never-signed PILOT is on the [Trinity Terrace page](/topics/trinity-terrace/).
