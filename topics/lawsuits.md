## Bachand v. Fitzgerald and the City — the election contest

FACT: Michael Bachand, who lost the Place 4 council race by 2 votes, is suing Council Member Robert Fitzgerald and the City, contesting the May 2 election — news reports describe the claim as ineligible votes being allowed. On June 18, the council voted 3–2 (the mayor breaking a 2–2 tie, Fitzgerald recused) to reimburse Fitzgerald's legal fees in the case. Background on [the election page](/westworth-is-watching/topics/election/), and [our notes from June 18](/westworth-is-watching/meetings/2026-06-18-special-session/) with the minutes linked.

FACT: Residents have filed public information requests for the legal opinions and invoices behind the city's involvement. What comes back gets posted here.

## The Burgess condemnation

FACT: The July 14 executive session list included "the Burgess condemnation" — a condemnation is the exercise of eminent domain, the government taking private property with compensation. That name on a closed-session list is, so far, everything the public record has told residents about it.

UNVERIFIED: We are not going to characterize whose land, what for, or on whose behalf until we have documents. Condemnation is a serious power, and claims about it get the same standard as everything else here. If you've received notice, seen a filing, or heard it discussed on the record, bring the specifics.

OPINION: If the city is taking private property — especially anywhere near a development project — that conversation belongs in open session, with the affected owners in the room, not on a closed-session line item.

## The criminal cases

FACT: The bribery case against Council Member Griffith — the pastry deliveries — was referred to the Tarrant County DA, and the Star-Telegram reported the DA would not pursue charges. The full story is on [the Pastry Gate page](/westworth-is-watching/topics/pastry-gate/).
