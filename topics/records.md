## How this went

FACT: Under the Texas Public Information Act, when a resident requests a public record, the city must produce it promptly — or, within ten business days, ask the Attorney General for permission to withhold it. "We'll release it later" is not one of the options the statute offers. Two requests sat unfulfilled anyway, until complaints were filed with the Attorney General's Open Records Division on August 7 ([OR-26-043635-IC](/files/oag-complaint-OR-26-043635-legal-opinion.pdf) and [OR-26-043636-IC](/files/oag-complaint-OR-26-043636-pilot.pdf)). Both documents have now been produced. Both requests are fulfilled — and both documents are posted below, in full, as promised.

## Document one: the legal opinion — and the action it doesn't cover

FACT: The city told residents the June 18 vote on Council Member Fitzgerald's legal fees rested on a written legal opinion, to be released "at a later time." Here it is: [the opinion of attorney Mollee Westfall of Cantey Hanger LLP](/files/westfall-opinion-2026-07-10.pdf), addressed to Mayor Jones — dated July 10, 2026, three weeks *after* the June 18 vote it discusses (its internal page headers read June 22).

FACT: Read what it actually concludes. The opinion says Westworth Village "is prohibited from defending a candidate's title to office," but may retain counsel to protect *the city's own interests* — chiefly, that if the election contest voids the result, the city would bear the cost of administering a second election. On that basis it concludes the city may retain and pay counsel.

FACT: Now read what the council actually voted. The official June 18 minutes record the motion as "reimbursement of legal fees to Council Member Robert Fitzgerald in the pending cause brought against him and the City" — with Fitzgerald recused and the mayor breaking a 2–2 tie. The opinion, by contrast, describes that vote as a vote "to retain private legal counsel" for the city. Those are two different actions, and only one of them appears in the minutes.

OPINION: This is the heart of it. If the city hires a lawyer to protect the city — the second-election cost, the city's own due process — the Westfall opinion supports that, and we take no issue with the principle. But that is not what the minutes say happened. The minutes say the city voted to reimburse *Mr. Fitzgerald's* legal fees in a suit over *his* seat — the exact thing the opinion concedes the city is "prohibited" from funding, and the exact thing the Attorney General has said public money can never do. The city appears to want to fund both interests with one vote, under an opinion that blesses only one of them. Which lawyer, representing whom, paid how much? That's what the still-pending request for [invoices](/topics/misuse-of-public-funds/) will show.

FACT: The opinion closes by suggesting the resident who requested it has a remedy: to "publicly protest this city council decision," vote accordingly, "or even run for office himself." This website is the first of those remedies in action.

## Document two: the PILOT — a draft, nothing more

FACT: The mayor has repeatedly claimed a PILOT (payment-in-lieu-of-taxes) agreement exists in connection with proposed development. What the city produced, after the AG complaint, is this: [a November 2022 email](/files/pilot-sample-request-2022-11-21.pdf) from the developer's advisor asking the city for "a sample of what you guys have used before for the PILOT program," and [a February 2023 email chain carrying a marked-up draft](/files/pilot-draft-redline-2023-02-28.pdf) — a redline comparison of two working versions of an "Agreement for Payments for Benefits Received," with 116 tracked changes and no signatures anywhere.

FACT: Nothing produced is signed, adopted, or final. And the draft was not included with the original planning and zoning submission for the project.

OPINION: A three-and-a-half-year-old unsigned redline is not an agreement. It's a conversation. When the village's biggest land-use decision gets discussed as if tax protection is locked in, residents deserve to know the "PILOT agreement" is, on the city's own production, a draft nobody ever signed.

## What we inferred, and what the record showed

FACT: When these documents were being withheld, we wrote — labeled as speculation — that the reasonable inference was that the PILOT didn't exist as a written agreement, and that the opinion was less favorable than claimed. The production bears out the first in full: draft only, never finalized. The second turned out more precise than we guessed: the opinion is genuinely favorable to something — just not to the thing the minutes say the council did. We said we'd post whatever came back, whatever it said. Here it is.
