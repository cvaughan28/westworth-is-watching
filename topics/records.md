## How this is supposed to work

FACT: Under the Texas Public Information Act, when a resident requests a public record, the city must produce it promptly — or, within ten business days, ask the Attorney General for permission to withhold it. "We'll release it later" is not one of the options the statute offers.

## Document one: the legal opinion behind the Fitzgerald payment

FACT: City officials have told residents that the June 18 vote to pay Council Member Fitzgerald's legal fees rests on a written legal opinion — identified to residents as authored by attorney Mollee Westfall. Residents have repeatedly requested that opinion under the Public Information Act. The city has not produced it, saying it will be released "at a later time."

FACT: On August 7, 2026, a complaint about that refusal was filed with the Attorney General's Open Records Division — service request OR-26-043635-IC. [Read the receipt](/files/oag-complaint-OR-26-043635-legal-opinion.pdf).

FACT: What the opinion needs to answer is not complicated. Two Attorney General opinions — [JM-685](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/opinions/47mattox/op/1987/pdf/jm0685.pdf) and [DM-431](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/opinions/48morales/op/1997/pdf/dm0431.pdf) — say public money can never pay an official's legal fees in an election contest, the second "as a matter of law." The full story is on [Misuse of Public Funds](/topics/misuse-of-public-funds/).

## Document two: the PILOT agreement

FACT: The mayor has repeatedly claimed that a PILOT (payment-in-lieu-of-taxes) agreement exists in connection with proposed development. A Public Information Act request for it has gone unfulfilled.

FACT: On August 7, 2026, a second complaint was filed with the Attorney General — service request OR-26-043636-IC. [Read the receipt](/files/oag-complaint-OR-26-043636-pilot.pdf).

FACT: Why it matters: Trinity Terrace's downtown campus is fully property-tax exempt, and a PILOT agreement is the standard tool a city uses to protect its tax base from exactly that. Whether one exists in writing — and what it says — is the whole ballgame for the [Kite Farm question](/topics/trinity-terrace/).

## What we make of it

SPECULATION: Ask the question the other way around: what would the city gain by sitting on either document? A favorable legal opinion would end the "misuse of public funds" argument overnight. A signed PILOT would answer the tax question in the city's favor. Records that help you are records you release. So we draw the reasonable inference — and label it as our inference — that the PILOT does not exist as a written agreement, and that the opinion is not as favorable as claimed. The city can prove us wrong any day it chooses, by doing what the law already requires.

FACT: The moment either document is produced, it will be posted here in full — whatever it says.
