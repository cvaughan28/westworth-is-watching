## Read it before Tuesday

FACT: The [agenda packet](https://www.cityofwestworth.com/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_08112026-200) was posted August 5. The items that matter most:

FACT: **The Gates at Westworth Village.** Public hearing and final council vote on the Shaddock Homes application to rezone 220 Redbird Lane from single-family SF-A to a gated-community Planned Development — the recommendation the [reconstituted P&amp;Z commission](/meetings/2026-08-04-pz-meeting/) issued at its first meeting. If you have a view on trading single-family zoning for a gated PD, this hearing is the moment the law sets aside for you to say it.

FACT: **The July 14 minutes** are up for approval on the consent agenda — the same minutes that record the [3–2 removal](/meetings/2026-07-14-council-meeting/) of three sitting P&amp;Z members with no cause stated.

FACT: **Your tax rate.** Preliminary action on a proposed FY 2026–27 rate of $0.475 per $100 valuation, with public hearings September 8 and September 15. Per the agenda, this budget would raise more total property taxes than last year's.

FACT: **Also:** a $384,501 city-hall green space contract recommended by the WRA, an ambulance-subscription ordinance, an election-services resolution — and another executive session on the Burgess condemnation, Digital Resources litigation, and (again) "board, committee, and commission appointments," followed by a CCPD appointments item.

FACT: Nothing anywhere on this agenda addresses the [August 4 demand letter](/files/demand-letter-pz-removal-2026-08-04.pdf), rescission of the July 14 removals, or the Open Meetings Act. The letter asked for a response within ten business days — that clock runs out August 18.

## Show up

FACT: Tuesday, August 11, 7:00 PM, Council Chambers, 311 Burton Hill Road. Citizen comments are near the start — submit a comment form in the lobby before they begin. Neighbors are having dinner first at Salsa Limón, 5012 White Settlement Rd, 5:30–6:45 PM.

OPINION: A full room on Tuesday tells the council that residents read the packet, noticed what's on it — and noticed what isn't.
