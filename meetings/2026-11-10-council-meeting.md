## What residents put on the record

FACT: By the close of the public hearing, residents had filed written protests from 46 of the 48 homes within 200 feet of the Kite Farm tract, and from 212 more households across the rest of the village, per the organizers' delivery records: 351 signatures in total, in a town of roughly 900 households. Residents also spoke against the project at the podium, as they had at the [November 3 P&amp;Z hearing](/protest/). The protest filings are public records held by the City Secretary.

## The recusal demand

FACT: On the day of the vote, counsel for the applicant delivered a demand that Council Member Halden Griffith recuse himself from the vote, asserting claims against both the city and Griffith personally if he participated. That account comes from residents with direct knowledge of the demand. Griffith recused and did not vote. The demand itself is a public record in the city's hands; a Public Information Act request for it is being filed, and the city's copy will be posted here when produced.

OPINION: A councilman with a family was given a choice on the morning of the vote: step aside, or carry personal legal risk for doing the job his neighbors elected him to do. He stepped aside. Whatever the legal merits of the demand, its timing did exactly one thing: it removed a likely no vote hours before the roll call.

## The vote

FACT: With Griffith recused, the council approved the Kite Farm rezoning 3 to 1: Poole, Dingman, and Fitzgerald in favor, Turner opposed. Debate from the members voting yes was minimal. The official minutes, once published, will be linked here with the adopted ordinance.

FACT: The protest numbers and the vote now sit side by side in the public record: 46 of 48 adjoining homes and 351 signatures against, and a 3 to 1 vote for.

## What's still open

UNVERIFIED: The adopted ordinance number, its effective date, and whether its final form contains the base zoning district, development standards, and attached concept plan that the city's code requires of a PD ordinance, defects [documented before the P&amp;Z hearing](/topics/trinity-terrace/). When the ordinance posts, it goes here.

FACT: The applicant's own page still says a PILOT agreement "would be established before development begins." No signed PILOT has ever been produced. That watch [continues](/topics/records/).

OPINION: The vote ended the rezoning question. It did not end anything else. Kelly Jones and Phillip Poole stand for election in May; Michael Dingman and Robert Fitzgerald face voters when their terms end. The record on this site is how a town remembers.
