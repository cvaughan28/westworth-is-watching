On the record

What happened

MAY 2, 2026

An election decided by 15, 24, and 2 votes

Three council seats were on the ballot. Place 2 was decided by 15 votes, Place 3 by about 24, and Place 4 by just 2. That same day, pastry boxes carrying campaign flyers showed up on doorsteps around the village — remember that detail. The election, in full.

MAY 16, 2026

A councilman arrested over pastries

Council Member Halden Griffith was arrested on a felony bribery allegation for delivering those pastry boxes, after the mayor reported him to police. The Star-Telegram later reported the Tarrant County DA would not pursue charges. Pastry Gate, start to finish.

JUN 18, 2026

City money for legal fees — on the mayor's tie-break

In a special session, the council voted to reimburse Council Member Fitzgerald's legal fees in the lawsuit contesting his 2-vote win. The vote tied 2–2; Mayor Jones broke the tie. Texas Attorney General opinions from 1987 and 1997 say public money can never pay an official's legal fees in an election contest — the 1997 opinion says never, "as a matter of law." The same night, the council made its board appointments — taking the man who filed that lawsuit off the P&Z Commission. Read our notes from that night.

JUL 14, 2026

Three more P&Z members removed — including the chair

With appointments already settled in June, the council removed three sitting P&Z members mid-term — among them the commission's chair, who had weeks earlier run against an incumbent. The official minutes, published August 5, show a motion to leave the boards alone failed 2–3 before the removals passed 3–2, with no cause stated. In one month, six of the commission's seven seats changed hands. Read our notes.

AUG 4, 2026

The demand letter — and the new commission's first vote

A formal demand was delivered to the city: rescind the July 14 removals, which violated the Open Meetings Act's notice requirements, or defend them in court. All three removed commissioners joined it. That evening, the reconstituted commission held its first meeting anyway — and recommended approval of its first rezoning, The Gates at Westworth Village. The full story.

AUG 7, 2026

Two withheld documents go to the Attorney General

The city says a written legal opinion justifies paying Fitzgerald's legal fees, and the mayor says a PILOT agreement exists for proposed development. It won't produce either one. Both refusals are now complaints before the Attorney General's Open Records Division. The records they won't release.

Our standard

What we can prove — and what we think

Documented

  • The June 18 minutes: legal fees approved 3–2 on the mayor's tie-breaking vote, with Fitzgerald recused
  • Attorney General opinions JM-685 and DM-431, in the AG's own words (linked below), which show that it is improper for the city to pay Robert Fitzgerald's legal fees
  • The official June 18 and July 14 minutes showing six of seven P&Z seats changed hands in a month — the July removals passing 3–2, no cause stated, over a motion to leave the boards alone
  • The city's written confirmation that "no notices were provided" to the commissioners it removed
  • The Griffith arrest, reported by the Fort Worth Report, KERA, WFAA, and CBS Texas
  • Cumberland Rest, Inc.'s tax-exempt status and its purchase of the 27-acre Kite Farm for a project reported at $350 million

Labeled opinion

  • Whether these actions were taken to clear a path for the Trinity Terrace rezoning is our speculation — but with the project still alive, it's a question every resident should be asking.
  • It is our understanding that a PILOT program (Payment in Lieu of Taxes) has never been a written agreement, just the potential to discuss a PILOT.
  • It's fair to question why the mayor is fighting so hard for a project that appears to be bad for our city, most of our residents don't want, and has already failed a rezoning request once.
