November 2026

To our neighbors

Last Tuesday night, four men rezoned the Kite Farm. I want to tell you what I'll remember about that night, and it isn't the vote.

Forty-six of the forty-eight homes within two hundred feet of that land signed formal protests. Two hundred twelve more households across the rest of the village signed alongside them. Three hundred fifty-one signatures, gathered door to door, porch to porch, neighbor to neighbor, in a town of twenty-five hundred people. Then you filled the chambers, twice, and stood at the podium, and said in your own words why single-family neighborhoods and a city that follows its own rules matter to you. Nobody organized us from outside. Nobody paid for a consultant. This town simply showed up for itself, in numbers Westworth Village has never seen.

And here is what it was up against. On the day of the vote, Trinity Terrace's lawyers delivered a demand that Councilman Griffith recuse himself, with threats aimed at the city and at him personally if he didn't. He has a family. He stepped aside rather than put them in the line of fire, and no one should have to make that choice for serving his neighbors. With him silenced, the rezoning passed 3 to 1. Councilmen Turner and Griffith fought to the last with everything the rules allowed them. The yes votes barely spoke. In our opinion, after everything documented on this site, the outcome was decided long before the hearing was ever opened.

So no, the signatures didn't stop it. But don't let anyone tell you they didn't count. Those 351 signatures are now a public record of what this town actually wants, held against a vote that gave it the opposite. A rezoning takes four votes on a Tuesday night. A council takes all of us, in May.

Kelly Jones and Phillip Poole are on the ballot this May. Michael Dingman and Robert Fitzgerald will face voters when their terms end. Remember the pastry arrest. Remember the legal fees you paid for a lawsuit the city wasn't party to. Remember six commission seats in thirty days, the chair removed without a word of cause, and a hearing where your 351 signatures were answered with near silence. Remember, and be there in May. We'll make sure none of them ever decides anything for this town again, the way towns like ours have always done it: by voting, together, in the open.

With more gratitude than I can fit on a webpage,
Your neighbors at Westworth Is Watching

On the record

What happened, in order

MAY 2, 2026

An election decided by 15, 24, and 2 votes

Three council seats were on the ballot; the closest was decided by 2 votes and is now in court. That same day, pastry boxes carrying campaign flyers landed on doorsteps. The election.

MAY 16, 2026

A councilman arrested over pastries

By the mayor's own account, he took resident reports, personally followed Council Member Griffith to collect evidence, and handed it to police. Griffith was arrested for felony bribery; the DA reportedly declined the case. Pastry Gate.

JUN 18, 2026

City money for legal fees, on the mayor's tie-break

The council voted 3 to 2, mayor deciding, to reimburse Fitzgerald's legal fees in the election contest. The city is not a party to that lawsuit. Misuse of public funds.

JUL 10, 2026

The city signs the engagement letter

The city signed with a Fort Worth law firm for the election contest. The letter names the city as the client and the only party that pays, and also names Councilman Fitzgerald as a jointly represented client. Residents wouldn't see it for three months. Who the lawyer actually represents.

JUL 14, 2026

Three more P&Z members removed, including the chair

A motion to leave the boards alone failed 2 to 3; the removals then passed 3 to 2, no cause stated. Six of seven seats changed hands in a month. Our notes.

AUG 4, 2026

The demand letter, and the new commission's first vote

A formal demand, joined by all three removed commissioners, asked the council to rescind the removals as an Open Meetings Act violation. That evening the reconstituted commission met anyway and advanced its first rezoning. Planning & Zoning.

AUG 7, 2026

Withheld records go to the Attorney General, and come out

Two complaints pried loose the legal opinion behind the fees vote (written three weeks after the vote) and the "PILOT agreement" (an unsigned 2023 draft). Both contradict the official story. Read them.

AUG 11, 2026

The 220 Redbird Lane rezoning is approved

Seven days after its first public hearing, the rebuilt commission's first recommendation became law: single-family to gated development, 5 to 0. Nothing on the agenda answered the demand letter. Our notes.

SEP 30, 2026

The Attorney General orders the city's records released

The city kept withholding its legal-fees records, then blew the deadlines for justifying it. The AG ruled it had "no choice but to order the requested information released." A week later, the engagement letter came out. The records fights.

OCT 1, 2026

Trinity Terrace is back

A new application to rezone the Kite Farm, three and a half years after the last one was voted down and withdrawn, and ten weeks after the commission that will hear it was rebuilt. Hearings: November 3 and November 10. The project, and how to protest.

OCT 7, 2026

The money question goes in writing

A records request for the payment records: what has actually been paid under the engagement letter, to whom, and whether Fitzgerald has reimbursed a cent. The city's deadline to respond is October 21. Document three.

NOV 1, 2026

The incompleteness case goes to every commissioner

A written comment delivered for the November 3 hearing lays out five defects, each cited to the city's own code: no deviations list, no owner signature, no survey, a draft ordinance missing everything a PD ordinance must contain, and no engineering review of this submittal. Under the code, incomplete applications "shall not be scheduled." The case, in full.

NOV 10, 2026

351 signatures against. Four votes for.

Protests from 46 of the 48 homes beside the tract and 212 more across town could not move it. After the applicant's day-of demand forced Councilman Griffith to recuse, the rezoning passed 3 to 1 with minimal debate. Our notes, and a letter to our neighbors.

Our standard

What we can prove, and what we think

Documented

  • The official June 18 and July 14 minutes: six of seven P&Z seats changed hands in a month, the July removals passing 3 to 2 over a motion to leave the boards alone, no cause stated
  • The city's written confirmation that "no notices were provided" to the commissioners it removed
  • The Westfall opinion, produced after an AG complaint and dated three weeks after the vote it justifies, conceding the city "is prohibited from defending a candidate's title to office"
  • The court file: the election contest names Robert Fitzgerald alone; the City is not a party
  • The mayor's support for the Trinity Terrace rezoning (Fort Worth Report and KERA coverage), beside his signed 2023 letter fighting a 2.95-acre apartment rezoning across the city line over traffic
  • Cumberland Rest, Inc.'s tax exemption and its purchase of the 27.73-acre Kite Farm for a project reported at $350 million

Labeled opinion

  • Whether these actions were taken to clear a path for the Trinity Terrace rezoning is our speculation. With the project still alive, it's a question every resident should be asking.
  • It is our understanding, now borne out by the city's own production, that a PILOT program (Payment in Lieu of Taxes) has never been a finalized agreement, just an unsigned draft and the potential to discuss one.
  • It's fair to question why the mayor is fighting so hard for a project that appears to be bad for our city, most of our residents don't want, and has already failed a rezoning request once.
